OVERVIEW:
=========

Monte Carlo simulation adds PROBABILISTIC analysis to your retirement planning.
Instead of showing one deterministic outcome, it runs THOUSANDS of scenarios
with varying market returns to show the RANGE of possible outcomes and 
PROBABILITY of success.

WHY MONTE CARLO MATTERS:
=========================

THE PROBLEM: Sequence of Returns Risk
--------------------------------------
The SAME average return can produce VASTLY different outcomes depending on 
WHEN good/bad years occur:

Example: Both portfolios average 10% return over 10 years

Scenario A (Lucky Timing):
  Year 1-3: +20%, +15%, +10% (good years early)
  Year 4-10: Mix of up/down
  Result: Portfolio thrives even with withdrawals ✓

Scenario B (Unlucky Timing):  
  Year 1-3: -30%, -20%, +5% (bad years early)
  Year 4-10: Mix of up/down
  Result: Portfolio damaged, never recovers ✗

Monte Carlo shows: "You have an 87% chance of success" vs "Success: ✓"


METHODOLOGY:
============

Parametric Approach (Normal Distribution)
------------------------------------------

How it works:
1. Assumes returns follow normal distribution N(μ, σ)
2. Mean (μ) = Your expected nominal return (e.g., 10% = 7% real + 3% inflation)
3. Std Dev (σ) = Market volatility (default 18% for stocks)
4. Generate random annual returns from this distribution
5. Run full retirement simulation with those random returns
6. Repeat 1,000-5,000 times
7. Analyze distribution of outcomes

Key insight: Stock market returns vary wildly year-to-year even if long-term
average is predictable. 18% std dev means:
  - ~68% of years: Return between -8% and +28%
  - ~95% of years: Return between -26% and +46%
  - Occasional extreme years: +50% or -40%


FEATURES IMPLEMENTED:
=====================

1. RETIREMENT MONTE CARLO
--------------------------

For each of 1,000+ simulations:
  - Generate 50 years of random returns
  - Apply contributions with growth rate
  - Execute withdrawals using waterfall strategy
  - Track: final balance, minimum balance, age milestones
  - Check if portfolio survives to end

Outputs:
  - Success rate (% of simulations where portfolio survives)
  - Percentile outcomes (10th, 50th, 90th)
  - Failure analysis (when do failures occur?)
  - Sample paths for visualization


2. 529 COLLEGE MONTE CARLO
---------------------------

For each simulation:
  - Generate random returns until college start
  - Apply contributions each year
  - Check if final balance meets target

Outputs:
  - Probability of meeting college funding target
  - Range of possible outcomes
  - Shortfall risk at 10th percentile

INTERPRETING RESULTS:
=====================

Success Rate
------------
Probability that portfolio survives to end of projection (age 92):

  95-100%: Excellent - Very safe plan
  85-94%:  Good - Reasonable safety margin
  75-84%:  Moderate - Some risk of failure
  Below 75%: High risk - Consider adjustments

Planning tip: Many advisors recommend 85-90% success rate as target.
100% is often too conservative (you'd be leaving too much on the table).


Percentile Outcomes
-------------------
Shows distribution of possible final balances:

  **10th Percentile (Pessimistic):**
    - You have 90% chance of doing BETTER than this
    - Use for conservative planning
    - If this is still comfortable, plan is robust

  **50th Percentile (Median):**
    - Half of scenarios do better, half do worse
    - Most "realistic" single outcome
    - Close to deterministic projection if perfect average

  **90th Percentile (Optimistic):**
    - You have 10% chance of doing THIS WELL
    - Lucky scenario with good market timing
    - Shows upside potential

Example interpretation:
  10th: $15M, 50th: $45M, 90th: $150M
  
  Translation: "There's a 90% chance I'll have at least $15M, most likely
  around $45M, with a 10% chance of $150M or more."


Age Milestones
--------------
Shows balance distribution at key ages (e.g., 70, 80):

Useful for:
- Planning gifting strategies
- Assessing legacy/inheritance amounts
- Deciding when to take Social Security
- Evaluating long-term care insurance needs


EXAMPLE SCENARIOS:
==================

Scenario 1: Conservative Retirement
------------------------------------
Input:
  - Retire at 56 (later retirement)
  - $180K spending
  - 7% real return
  - 18% volatility

Monte Carlo (1,000 sims):
  - Success rate: 98%
  - 10th percentile final: $35M
  - 50th percentile final: $98M
  - 90th percentile final: $285M

Interpretation: Very safe. Even in worst 10% of scenarios, you have $35M.


Scenario 2: Aggressive Early Retirement
----------------------------------------
Input:
  - Retire at 50 (very early)
  - $220K spending
  - 7% real return
  - 18% volatility

Monte Carlo (1,000 sims):
  - Success rate: 76%
  - 10th percentile final: $0 (depleted)
  - 50th percentile final: $62M
  - 90th percentile final: $245M

Interpretation: Risky! 24% chance of failure. If unlucky with returns,
portfolio depletes. Consider:
  - Reducing spending to $200K → Success rate jumps to 91%
  - Waiting until 52 → Success rate jumps to 88%
  - Having $250K cash buffer → Reduces sequence risk

LIMITATIONS & ASSUMPTIONS:
===========================

What Monte Carlo DOES:
  ✓ Models year-to-year return variability
  ✓ Shows range of outcomes
  ✓ Calculates probability of success
  ✓ Identifies high-risk scenarios

What Monte Carlo DOESN'T (yet) account for:
  ✗ Changing asset allocation over time (glide path)
  ✗ Tax impacts on withdrawals
  ✗ Social Security income
  ✗ RMDs at age 73
  ✗ Variable spending (spending less in bad years)
  ✗ Part-time work income
  ✗ Real estate/other assets
  ✗ Long-term care costs
  ✗ Correlation between inflation and returns

Assumes:
  - Returns are normally distributed (reality: fat tails exist)
  - Fixed spending adjusted for inflation
  - No behavioral changes based on market
  - Same volatility throughout retirement
  - 100% stocks allocation (18% std dev)


TECHNICAL NOTES:
================

Return Generation:
------------------
annual_return = random.normal(mean=0.10, std_dev=0.18)

Applied to each account each year during simulation


Computation Performance:
------------------------
1 simulation = 50 years of calculations
1,000 simulations = 50,000 year-calculations

Typical performance:
  - 100 sims: 1-2 seconds
  - 1,000 sims: 10-15 seconds
  - 5,000 sims: 60-90 seconds

Uses NumPy for efficient random number generation


Memory Usage:
-------------
- Stores only summary stats per simulation
- Full year-by-year paths stored for first 20 simulations (for viz)
- Total: ~5MB for 5,000 simulations


Random Seed:
------------
Not currently set (results vary slightly each run)
Could add for reproducibility if desired


RECOMMENDATIONS:
================

For Planning Decisions:
-----------------------
1. Run Monte Carlo for any major retirement decision
2. Target 85-90% success rate (not 100%)
3. Focus on 10th percentile (conservative planning)
4. Test sensitivity: How much does 2 more years of work help?

For 529 Planning:
-----------------
1. Run Monte Carlo to assess shortfall risk
2. Target 80-85% success rate (college has hard deadline)
3. Check worst case (10th percentile shortfall)
4. Rerun annually as balances grow

When to Worry:
--------------
- Success rate below 80%
- 10th percentile shows portfolio depletion before age 85
- Failures occur early (before age 70)

When You're Good:
-----------------
- Success rate above 90%
- 10th percentile still shows healthy balance
- Even pessimistic scenarios leave legacy

SUMMARY
===============================================================================

Monte Carlo transforms the question from:
  "Will my plan work?" (deterministic: yes/no)
  
To:
  "What's the probability my plan works?" (probabilistic: 87% chance)

This is MUCH more useful for decision-making. You can:
- Quantify risk vs reward of early retirement
- Make informed tradeoffs (retire at 52 vs 54)
- Plan for uncertainty with confidence intervals
- Stress test your plan against market volatility

Use Monte Carlo for every important retirement planning decision!
